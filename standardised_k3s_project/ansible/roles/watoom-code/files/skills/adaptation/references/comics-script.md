# Comics and graphic novel writing

Use for original comics, manga, graphic novels, webcomics and adaptations.
Genre and visual style are separate choices: manga need not be right-to-left;
a superhero story need not use a particular rendering style. Preserve the
author's choices and target audience. This workflow is a practical synthesis,
not a mandatory industry format; see graphic-novel-sources.md.

## Choose a finishable deliverable

Record premise, protagonist's desire, obstacle, stakes, emotional change and
ending. For an adaptation, identify what to keep, combine, cut or externalize
without altering the source manuscript. A standalone original needs no prose
novel first. Outline scene purpose and changes in knowledge/emotion; structure
models are options rather than requirements.

Agree a page/episode budget, scope and next finishable milestone. If useful,
finish a short complete sequence before scaling to a long book; do not
silently shrink a requested book to a sample. Keep a simple page ledger for
script, thumbnail, pencils, inks, color/tones, lettering, description and review.
Avoid indefinitely polishing the opening while later pages remain undrafted.

Choose full script, thumbnail-first, screenplay/hybrid with page breaks, or
plot-first according to creator/collaborator preference. Full script is a useful
fallback for a writer-to-artist handoff. Do not impose it on an author-artist.
Script page count is not a reliable conversion to finished comic page count.

## Project location and continuity

Inside a story project use adaptations/comics/ and preserve character/location/
scene ids. In an original standalone project use comics/ or the existing
author folder. Do not invent Story CLI commands or graphic-novel form values.
The CLI does not validate comics scripts or deliver finished illustrated pages.

Keep a compact visual bible: character ids, distinguishing features, proportions,
wardrobe versions, props, locations, relative heights, palette and line/texture
rules. Track injuries, handedness, time, weather and object positions across
panels. Record deliberate changes rather than treating every difference as error.

## Page and spread plan

Budget pages by scene before fine scripting. For each page record scene purpose,
entry and exit beats, panel count, dialogue load and reveal/setup.
Consider the facing spread as a unit. In a conventional left-to-right book whose
first story page is recto, odd pages are right and even pages left. Check actual
front matter, binding and numbering before assigning turns. After a turn the
whole next spread can be visible: protect the reveal from being seen early.
Do not promise a surprise solely from odd/even numbering. Vertical scroll needs
screenful/spacing reveals rather than print page-turn parity.

## Panels, clarity and pacing

Stage who acts, where they stand, what changes and what the reader must notice.
Use a drawable decisive instant per ordinary panel; split sequential actions.
Explicit continuous-narrative or repeated-figure panels are deliberate exceptions.
Show thought through behavior, contrast or necessary captions rather than
requiring an artist to draw an invisible abstract idea.

For left-to-right pages, begin with a clear top-left to bottom-right reading
route. Adapt deliberately for right-to-left and vertical reading. Reserve balloon
space in thumbnails, identify balloon order and speaker tails, and avoid
ambiguous gutters, crossed tails or action pointing into the wrong panel.
Favor readable geography and silhouettes over spectacle that obscures events.

Vary establishing wides, interaction mediums and emotional/detail close-ups
where they serve the beat. Re-establish after a location/geography change;
not every page needs a redundant wide shot. Preserve eyelines and movement
direction or show a deliberate transition.

Panel area, number, repetition, gutters, text density and visual complexity all
affect pacing. A small panel can be a quick reaction; several small panels can
also stretch a second into a long suspense beat. A large panel can invite
lingering or deliver a sudden impact. Judge the sequence at reading size instead
of enforcing small=fast or large=slow. Use silent beats and image/text tension.
Comics need not behave like film storyboards.

## Script and lettering handoff

Give explicit page and panel ids/counts. For each panel supply the shot when
useful, visible action, emotion, essential staging/continuity and ordered
CAPTION, CHARACTER dialogue and SFX. Mark OFF-PANEL, WHISPER, RADIO and other
delivery only when meaningful. Separate production notes from printed text.
Keep source scene ids where adapting; leave nonessential composition to the
illustrator. Templates are in graphic-novel-templates.md.

Treat roughly 20-25 words per balloon and 150-200 words per page as early
crowding warnings, not universal limits or a target. Audience, trim, font and
art determine the actual fit. Test balloons at final reading size; cut or
redistribute dialogue before shrinking text or hiding faces and action.
Caption narration should add information rather than redundantly describe art.

## Keep illustration notes while writing

For every page drafted or revised, create/update its persistent page-art note
before calling that writing milestone complete. Derive the required images from
what happens in the story: story purpose, visible beat, emotion, staging,
character/wardrobe state, essential props, visual clues and continuity.
Use graphic-novel-art-notes.md for file locations, revision rules and the art
manifest. Keep exact printed text separate. Update dependent notes after story
changes and mark affected image assets stale; keep unaffected work reusable.
A later illustration request uses these saved notes, not chat-memory recall.

## Review and completion

Read script and thumbnails together for continuity, emotional logic, drawable
action, panel/balloon order, spread spoilers and dialogue fit. Finish a complete
draft at the agreed scope, revise weak sequences, then move through the art
stages in graphic-novel-illustration.md. Keep completed work and outstanding
pages visible. Distinguish SCRIPTED, THUMBNAILED, ART_COMPLETE, LETTERED,
REVIEWED and EXPORTED; do not label prompts or prose as illustrated pages.
